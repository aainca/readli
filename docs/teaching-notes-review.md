## 1.1 Closed (short vowel)

## Rule
A vowel closed in by a consonant says its short sound: a as in cat, i as in pin.

## How to teach
1. Write "cat". Cover the t with your finger and say "the a is trapped by the t, so it stays short."
2. Read cat, map, pen, sun together, tapping each sound.
3. Let him read the next five alone.

## What to say
"Is there a consonant after the vowel? Then the vowel stays short."
"Tap it out: c, a, t."

## Common mistakes
He says the long sound (cape for cap). Do not say the word. Point to the last consonant and ask what is trapping the vowel.
He guesses from the first letter. Ask him to tap every sound.

## Activity
Write a, e, i, o, u on sticky notes. Say a short vowel sound; he grabs the right note and names a word.

## Ready when
He reads new short vowel words on the first try without tapping.

## 1.2 Open (long vowel)

## Rule
A syllable that ends in a vowel is open, so the vowel says its name: go, me, ro-bot.

## How to teach
1. Write "go". Point to the end and say "nothing comes after the o, so it can say its name."
2. Compare "got" and "go". Cover the t and watch the vowel change.
3. Read no, so, hi, we together, then he tries she, fly, my.

## What to say
"What is at the end of the syllable? A vowel? Then it says its name."
"Open door, long sound."

## Common mistakes
He reads "go" as "gah". Ask what comes after the o. Nothing, so it is open.
In ro-bot he closes the first part. Cover "bot" and read "ro" alone.

## Activity
Write five words on paper: not, hop, bit, met, cut. He erases the last letter and reads the open word that is left.

## Ready when
He reads new open syllables with the long sound without stopping to think.

## 1.3 Silent e

## Rule
An e at the end of a word is silent and makes the vowel before it say its name.

## How to teach
1. Write "cap". Have him read it.
2. Add an e and say "the e is quiet, but it makes the a say its name." Read "cape" together.
3. Repeat with pin and pine, hop and hope. Then let him do the next pair alone.

## What to say
"Is there an e at the end? Then what does the vowel say?"
"Silent e, long vowel."

## Common mistakes
He reads "cape" as "cap". Do not say the word. Point to the e and ask the question above.
He says the e out loud (cap-ee). Remind him the e is quiet.

## Activity
Write 5 short words on paper (tap, kit, not, cub, man). He adds an e to each and reads both.

## Ready when
He reads new silent e words correctly on the first try without pointing to the e.

## 1.4 Vowel teams

## Rule
Two vowels together make one sound, usually the first one's name: rain, feet, boat.

## How to teach
1. Write "rain". Circle "ai" and say "these two work as a team. The first one talks, the second one is quiet."
2. Read rain, pain, mail together, then boat, coat, road. Keep one team per round.
3. Let him read five new words alone before switching teams.

## What to say
"Find the team. Which vowel is first? That is the sound."
"When two vowels go walking, the first one does the talking."

## Common mistakes
He reads only the first vowel as short (ran for rain). Ask him to find the team and circle it.
He says both vowels. A team makes one sound.

## Activity
Write ai, ee, oa on paper. He hunts for words around the house (cereal box, mail) that use each team.

## Ready when
He spots the team before reading and gets new team words right on the first try.

## 1.5 R-controlled (bossy r)

## Rule
An r right after a vowel changes the vowel's sound: car, fork, her, bird, turn.

## How to teach
1. Write "cat" and "car". Read both. Say "the r is bossy. It changes the a."
2. Practice ar words, then or words. Save er, ir, ur for later; they sound the same.
3. Let him read five new words with one r sound alone.

## What to say
"Is there an r after the vowel? Then the r is the boss."
"Say the vowel and the r together, as one sound."

## Common mistakes
He reads the vowel short and adds r (cuh-ar). Ask for the vowel and r as one chunk.
He mixes up er, ir, ur. That is fine; they sound the same.

## Activity
Write ar, or, er, ir, ur on five cards. Say a word (farm, corn, bird). He holds up the card that matches.

## Ready when
He reads new car, corn and bird-type words smoothly on the first try.

## 1.6 Consonant-le

## Rule
A consonant plus le at the end makes its own last syllable: ta-ble, can-dle.

## How to teach
1. Write "table". Count back three letters from the end and draw a line: ta | ble.
2. Say "ble says bul. Now read the first part." It is open (ta) if it ends with a vowel, closed (can) if not.
3. Do candle, puzzle, apple together, then let him split and read two alone.

## What to say
"Count back three. Draw the line. Read the first part, then add the ending."

## Common mistakes
He says the e (tab-lee). Remind him the ending just says bul, dul, zul.
He reads the first part wrong. Cover the ending and ask if the first part is open or closed.

## Activity
Write table, candle, apple, puzzle, turtle on paper. He draws each split line and reads them.

## Ready when
He splits new consonant-le words himself and reads them on the first try.

## 1.7 Diphthongs

## Rule
Some vowel pairs make a gliding sound: oi and oy as in coin and boy, ou and ow as in out and cow.

## How to teach
1. Write "boy". Say it slowly so he feels his mouth move. "Two vowels, one sliding sound."
2. Practice oi and oy together (coin, boy, toy, join), then ou and ow (out, cow, loud, town).
3. Let him read five new words alone.

## What to say
"Find the pair. Does your mouth slide? That is the gliding sound."

## Common mistakes
He reads ow as in snow instead of cow. Have him try the other sound and pick the real word.
He reads only one vowel. Ask him to find the pair first.

## Activity
Say a word. He answers "oi" or "ow" and points left or right. Mix in plain words (cat) he should not point for.

## Ready when
He reads new oi, oy, ou and ow words correctly on the first try.

## 2.1 Soft c and g

## Rule
Before e, i or y, c says s (cent, city) and g says j (gem, page).

## How to teach
1. Write "cat" and "cent". Say "look at the letter after the c. An e, i or y makes it soft."
2. Do the same with "gate" and "gem".
3. Read cent, ice, city, then gem, page, giant together. Let him try five alone.

## What to say
"What letter comes after the c? e, i or y? Then it says s. Same for g: it says j."

## Common mistakes
He reads "cent" with a k sound. Do not say the word. Point to the letter after the c and ask.
He forgets y counts. Remind him of the three signals.

## Activity
Write ten c and g words on cards. He sorts them into hard and soft piles, then reads each pile.

## Ready when
He checks the next letter himself and reads new soft c and g words on the first try.

## Syllable splitting (coaching the split)

## Rule
Long words are read in parts. Find the vowels, count the consonants between them, and split so each part has one vowel sound.

## How to teach
1. Write "rabbit". Have him dot the vowels. Two consonants between them? Split in the middle: rab-bit.
2. Write "robot". One consonant between the vowels? Try splitting before it first: ro-bot. If that sounds wrong (cab-in), split after it.
3. Read each part, then blend. On the next word, only point.

## What to say
"Find the vowels. How many consonants in between? Where does it split? Read each part. Now blend it."

## Common mistakes
He guesses from the first letters. Cover all but the first part.
The blended word sounds wrong. Say "try the other vowel sound" and let him fix it himself.

## Activity
Write napkin, tiger, candle on paper. He dots the vowels, draws the split, and reads them.

## Ready when
He splits a new two-syllable word himself and reads it without help.
